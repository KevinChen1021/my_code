• Today, I own 200 shares of DELL stocks worth 
$2,942. The maximum loss tomorrow is $239 with a 
99% confidence level.
• Our mutual fund has a value of $10m today. The 
maximum loss over the next 3 months is 0.5m with 
95% confidence. 
• The value of our bank is $150m. The VaR of our bank 
is $10m with 99% confidence over the next 6 
months.

--- PAGE 20 ---
stats.shapiro test (Shapiro-Wilk test)
• Hypothesis
• Null Hypothesis: The data is normally distributed.
• Alternative Hypothesis: The data is not normally distributed. 
• Interpreting Results
• The test outputs a statistic and a p-value. 
• p > 0.05: Fail to reject the null hypothesis. The data is likely drawn from a 
normal distribution.
• p <= 0.05: Reject the null hypothesis. There is a statistically significant 
deviation from normality.
--- PAGE 21 ---
stats.anderson test (Anderson-Darling test)
• Hypothesis
• Null Hypothesis: The data comes from the specified distribution.
• Alternative Hypothesis: The data does not come from the specified 
distribution.
• Interpreting Results
• The test outputs a statistic and a p-value. 
• p > 0.05: Fail to reject the null hypothesis. The data is likely drawn from a 
specified distribution.
• p <= 0.05: Reject the null hypothesis. There is a statistically significant 
deviation from a specified distribution.

Volatility of a 2-stock portfolio
# volatility of two-stock portfolio
import numpy as np
#
def portfolio_vol_2stocks(ret1,ret2,w1):
     x1=w1
     x2=1-x1
     s1=ret1.std()
     s2=ret2.std()
     v=x1**2*s1**2+x2**2*s2**2+2*x1*x2*np.cov(ret1,ret2)
     return(sqrt(v))

VaR for portfolios
import numpy as np
from math import sqrt
def vol_portfolio(ret_matrix,weights):
    m=ret_matrix.shape[1]
    m2=len(weights)
    if(m!=m2):
        print("# of stocks  not = # of weights!")
    else:
        cov=np.cov(ret_matrix.T)
        final=0
        n=np.arange(0,m)
        for i in n:
            for j in n:
                final+=weights[i]*weights[j]*cov[i,j]
    stdP=sqrt(final)
    return(stdP)

import numpy as np
import scipy.stats as stats 
from math import log,exp,sqrt
import numpy.random as random
#
def bsCall(S,X,T,r,sigma):
    d1=(log(S/X)+(r+sigma*sigma/2.)*T)/(sigma*sqrt(T)) 
    d2 = d1-sigma*sqrt(T)
    c=S*stats.norm.cdf(d1)-X*exp(-r*T)*stats.norm.cdf(d2)
    return(c) 
#
def up_and_out_call(s0,x,T,r,sigma,n_simulation,barrier):
    n_steps=100 
    dt=T/n_steps 
    total=0 
    for j in np.arange(0, n_simulation): 
        sT=s0 
        out=False
        for i in np.arange(0,int(n_steps)): 
            e=random.normal() 
            sT*=exp((r-0.5*sigma*sigma)*dt+sigma*e*sqrt(dt)) 
            if sT>barrier: 
               out=True 
        if out==False: 
            total+=bsCall(s0,x,T,r,sigma) 
    return(total/n_simulation)

What are exotic options?
• Exotic options are a class of derivative contracts that possess 
features or structures that differ from standard or vanilla options.
•  These options are typically tailored to meet specific needs or to 
hedge against particular risks that may not be adequately 
addressed by standardized options contracts traded on 
exchanges. 
• Exotic options often offer greater flexibility and customization but 
may also involve more complex payoff structures and pricing 
mechanisms.
--- PAGE 79 ---
Some common types of exotic options
1. Asian Options: The payoff of an Asian option depends on the average price of the 
underlying asset over a specified period rather than the asset's price at expiration.
2. Barrier Options: Barrier options come into existence or cease to exist if the price of 
the underlying asset reaches a predetermined barrier level during the life of the 
option. Examples include knock-in and knock-out options.
3. Binary Options: Binary options have a fixed payoff determined at the outset of the 
contract, depending on whether the underlying asset's price reaches a certain level or 
not.
4. Lookback Options: Lookback options provide the holder with the right to buy or sell 
the underlying asset at its highest or lowest price during the option's life.
--- PAGE 80 ---
Some common types of exotic options (2)
5. Compound Options: Compound options are options on other options. They 
provide the holder with the right to buy or sell another option at a 
predetermined price and date.
6. Chooser Options: Chooser options allow the holder to choose whether the 
option will be a call or put at a specified future date.
7. Bermudan Options: Bermudan options can be exercised at predetermined 
dates before expiration, similar to American options, but typically with limited 
exercise dates.
8. Quanto Options: Quanto options are denominated in one currency but 
settled in another, mitigating currency risk.

Exotic options

before the maturity date, it is called a Bermudan option. Many exotic 
options (derivatives) may have several triggers relating to their payoffs. 
An exotic option may also include non-standard underlying security or 
instrument, developed for a specific client or for a particular market. 
Exotic options are often traded over-the-counter (OTC) rather than on 
organized exchanges, and their prices may be determined through complex

management needs commonly use them.

Barrier options
Unlike the Black-Scholes-Merton option model's call and put options, which are 
path-independent, a barrier option is path-dependent. A barrier option is similar in 
many ways to an ordinary option, except a trigger exists. An in option starts its life 
worthless unless the underlying stock reaches a predetermined knock-in barrier. 
On the contrary, an out barrier option starts its life active and turns useless when a 
knock-out barrier price is breached. In addition, if a barrier option expires inactive, 
it may be worthless, or there may be a cash rebate paid out as a fraction of the 
premium. 
The four types of barrier options are given in the next slide. 
--- PAGE 85 ---
Four types of barrier-options
• Up-and-out: In this barrier option, the price starts from below a barrier level. 
If it reaches the barrier, it is knocked out.
• Down-and-out: In this barrier option, the price starts from above a barrier. If it 
reaches the barrier, it is knocked out.
• Up-and-in: In this barrier option, the price starts down a barrier and has to 
reach the barrier to be activated.
• Down-and-in: In this barrier option, the price starts over a barrier and has to 
reach the barrier to be activated

What are the chooser options?
Chooser options, also known as "extendible options" or "delayed 
options, " are a type of exotic option that grants the holder the right 
to choose the type of option (call or put) they wish to exercise at a 
predetermined future date. 
This additional flexibility distinguishes chooser options from 
standard options, where the type of option (call or put) is fixed at 
the time of purchase.
--- PAGE 93 ---
The key features of chooser options
1. Flexibility: Chooser options allow the holder to assess market conditions 
and select the most advantageous option type (call or put) at a later date. This 
flexibility can be valuable in uncertain market environments or when the holder 
anticipates significant price movements but is unsure about the direction.
2. Exercise Period: The holder of a chooser option has the right to choose the 
option type (call or put) during a specified exercise period, which typically 
occurs before the option's expiration date. The exercise period may be a single 
date or a range of dates.
3. Premium: Chooser options generally command a higher premium compared 
to standard options due to the additional flexibility they offer. The premium is 
influenced by factors such as the underlying asset's volatility, time to 
expiration, and the length of the exercise period.
--- PAGE 94 ---
The key features of chooser options (2)
4. Expiration: Chooser options have a fixed expiration date, like standard 
options. The holder must exercise the option within the specified 
exercise period or let it expire worthless.
5. Risk Management: Chooser options can be used for risk management 
purposes, allowing the holder to adapt their option position based on 
evolving market conditions and their outlook for the underlying asset.
6. Complexity: Due to their added flexibility, chooser options are 
considered more complex than standard options. As a result, they are 
typically traded over-the-counter (OTC) rather than on organized 
exchanges and may require customized terms to meet the specific 
needs of the parties involved.
